# Next steps

Project Ludlow never files anything and never contacts your employer. Everything in this package is a draft for people to review.

1. **Talk to someone experienced first.** Have a labor attorney or an experienced organizer read this package. TODO(lawyer): confirm every step below is current.
2. **Decide your route.**
   - *Ask for voluntary recognition.* Send the recognition letter. The employer may say yes, say no, or ignore it. The law about what an employer must do after a card majority is unsettled (see the note in the letter).
   - *Ask the NLRB for an election.* File a petition (an "RC" petition). The NLRB requires a "showing of interest" from at least 30% of the employees in the unit. The cards in this package are that showing.
3. **Electronic cards need paperwork.** NLRB GC Memo 15-08 (Revised, Oct. 26, 2015) says electronic signatures can support the showing of interest if the card includes the signer's name, contact information, telephone number, the language agreed to, the date, and the employer's name; the filer submits a declaration about the signature technology; and, for simple e-signatures, a Confirmation Transmission was sent to the signer. The declaration draft in this package covers that. Cards must not include dates of birth, Social Security numbers or other sensitive identifiers.
4. **Check your reply inbox.** Replies to the confirmation emails go to the address your committee set. Any responses received by the time you file must also be given to the NLRB.
5. **Who sees what.** The showing of interest goes to the NLRB. It is an administrative matter and is not shared with the employer. The petition itself must be served on the employer.
