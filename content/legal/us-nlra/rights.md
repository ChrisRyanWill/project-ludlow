# Your rights, in plain words

Under Section 7 of the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA), most private-sector workers in the United States have the right to talk with coworkers about pay and working conditions, to join or support a union, and to act together to improve their jobs. It is illegal for an employer to punish you for using these rights.

## What an employer may not do
Organizers remember four things with the word **TIPS**:

- **Threaten** you, for example with firing, cuts in hours or closing.
- **Interrogate** you about whether you support a union or who does.
- **Promise** raises or perks to make you drop the union.
- **Spy** on union activity, or make it look like they are.

They also may not fire, discipline or treat you worse because of union activity.

## Who is not covered by the NLRA
Supervisors and managers, independent contractors, agricultural workers, domestic workers in private homes, and most government employees are not covered by the NLRA. Railroad and airline workers are covered by a different law, the Railway Labor Act. Some of these groups have other rights under state law or other laws.

## If something happens
1. Stay calm. Do not argue on the job.
2. Write down what happened, when, and who saw it (use your private record in Project Ludlow).
3. Tell your organizing committee.
4. Ask about a charge with the NLRB: nlrb.gov. There are time limits, so do not wait.

Learn more at [nlrb.gov](https://www.nlrb.gov).
