# Next steps

Project Ludlow does not know the law where you live, so this list is general. Do not skip step 1.

1. **Find local help.** Contact an established union, a workers' center or a labor lawyer where you live. Ask: "What do we need to be legally recognised, and what protects us while we organize?"
2. **Learn the threshold.** Many countries set a minimum share of workers who must show support before the law requires the employer to deal with you. Ask what it is. Enter it in your campaign settings if you have not already, so the progress bar can show it.
3. **Learn the process.** Ask who decides recognition (a labor board, a court, a registrar) and what proof they accept.
4. **Aim high before you go public.** Organizers often try to reach about two thirds support before the employer knows, because some support fades once the employer starts campaigning.
5. **Write down everything.** If anyone is threatened or treated worse because of union activity, record what happened, when and who saw it. Use your private record in Project Ludlow.
6. **Review everything with a local expert** before you send or file anything.

International labor standards (ILO Conventions 87 and 98) protect the right to organize and to bargain collectively, and most countries have signed them. Your local law decides how those rights work in practice.
